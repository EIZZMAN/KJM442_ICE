Heat Transfer Mechanisms

Modes of Heat Transfer

Thermal Conduction

Conduction is the transfer of energy from more energetic particles to less energetic ones as a result of interaction between particles.

Fourier's Law:

Q = -k A (dT/dx)

k = thermal conductivity (W/m.K)

Thermal Convection

Convection occurs between a solid surface and an adjacent fluid that is in motion.

Newton's Law of Cooling:

Q = h A (Ts - T∞)

h = convection heat transfer coefficient (W/m².K)

Thermal Radiation

Radiation is energy emitted by matter in the form of electromagnetic waves.

Stefan-Boltzmann Law:

Q = ε σ A Ts⁴

Power Cycles & Engines

Internal Combustion Engines

What is an ICE?

A heat engine where the combustion of a fuel occurs with an oxidizer (usually air) in a combustion chamber that is an integral part of the working fluid flow circuit.

Compression Ignition (CI)

Engine Basics

Internal Combustion Engines (ICE) are categorized primarily by their ignition method and cycle strokes. In Spark Ignition (SI) engines, a fuel-air mixture is ignited by a spark plug. In Compression Ignition (CI) engines, air is compressed to high temperatures before fuel is injected and self-ignites.

  • High power-to-weight ratio
  • Fast starting and acceleration
  • Versatile fuel compatibility (Gasoline, Diesel, LNG)

P-v Diagram: Ideal Otto Cycle

Ideal Otto Cycle

The ideal cycle for spark-ignition (SI) engines. It consists of four internally reversible processes:

  1. Isentropic Compression (1-2)
  2. Constant Volume Heat Addition (2-3)
  3. Isentropic Expansion (3-4)
  4. Constant Volume Heat Rejection (4-1)

Thermal Efficiency (η)

ηth,Otto = 1 - (1 / rk-1)

r = compression ratio, k = specific heat ratio

P-v Diagram: Ideal Diesel Cycle

Ideal Diesel Cycle

The ideal cycle for compression-ignition (CI) engines. Unlike the Otto cycle, heat is added at constant pressure.

  1. Isentropic Compression (1-2)
  2. Constant Pressure Heat Addition (2-3)
  3. Isentropic Expansion (3-4)
  4. Constant Volume Heat Rejection (4-1)

Thermal Efficiency (η)

η = 1 - [ (1/rk-1) * (rck - 1)/(k(rc-1)) ]

rc = cutoff ratio, r = compression ratio

1. Study Notes: Thermodynamic Cycles

Otto Cycle vs. Diesel Cycle Analysis

Understanding the P-V diagram is crucial for evaluating engine performance. The primary distinction lies in how heat is added to the system (Process 2 $\rightarrow$ 3).

Feature Otto Cycle (SI Engine) Diesel Cycle (CI Engine)
Heat Addition (2 $\rightarrow$ 3) Occurs at Constant Volume ($V=C$). Represented by a vertical line. Occurs at Constant Pressure ($P=C$). Represented by a horizontal line.
Ignition Type Spark Ignition (SI) via Spark Plug. Compression Ignition (CI) via high pressure.
Compression Ratio ($r$) Lower ($r \approx 7-10$) to prevent knocking. Higher ($r \approx 14-22$) to ensure auto-ignition.
Efficiency Formula ($\eta$) $\eta_{otto} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{k-1}}$ $\eta_{diesel} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{k-1}} \left[ \frac{\rho^k - 1}{k(\rho - 1)} \right]$
Otto Cycle Stages
  • 1 $\rightarrow$ 2: Isentropic Compression
  • 2 $\rightarrow$ 3: Constant Volume Heat Addition ($Q_{in}$)
  • 3 $\rightarrow$ 4: Isentropic Expansion (Power Stroke)
  • 4 $\rightarrow$ 1: Constant Volume Heat Rejection ($Q_{out}$)
Diesel Cycle Stages
  • 1 $\rightarrow$ 2: Isentropic Compression
  • 2 $\rightarrow$ 3: Constant Pressure Heat Addition ($Q_{in}$)
  • 3 $\rightarrow$ 4: Isentropic Expansion (Power Stroke)
  • 4 $\rightarrow$ 1: Constant Volume Heat Rejection ($Q_{out}$)
